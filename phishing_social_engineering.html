Phishing & Social Engineering 🎣

Learn how attackers manipulate people into revealing information or performing unsafe actions, and how to recognize and prevent these attacks.

🎣

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering technique in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick someone into revealing sensitive information or clicking a malicious link.

Phishing commonly happens through email, messages, websites and other communication channels.

What is Social Engineering?

Social engineering is the use of psychological manipulation to influence people into revealing information or taking an action that may reduce their security.

Instead of attacking only technology, attackers may target human trust, fear, curiosity or urgency.

Common Types of Phishing

  • Email Phishing – Fake emails that appear to come from trusted organizations.
  • Spear Phishing – A targeted phishing attempt aimed at a specific person or organization.
  • Smishing – Phishing attacks delivered through SMS or messaging services.
  • Vishing – Fraudulent phone calls designed to trick victims.
  • Website Phishing – Fake websites designed to look like legitimate websites.

Common Social Engineering Techniques 🧠

  • Urgency – Creating pressure so the person acts quickly.
  • Fear – Making the victim believe something bad will happen.
  • Impersonation – Pretending to be a trusted person or organization.
  • Authority – Using the appearance of authority to gain trust.
  • Curiosity – Using interesting messages or files to encourage unsafe actions.

Warning Signs of Phishing ⚠️

  • Unexpected messages asking for sensitive information.
  • Urgent requests demanding immediate action.
  • Suspicious links or unexpected attachments.
  • Unknown or unusual sender addresses.
  • Spelling or grammar mistakes in suspicious messages.
  • Requests for passwords, OTPs or financial information.
  • Messages claiming your account will be closed immediately.

How to Identify a Suspicious Link 🔗

Before clicking a link, carefully check the destination and the domain name.

  • Check the spelling of the domain.
  • Be careful with shortened or unexpected links.
  • Do not trust a link simply because it uses a familiar logo.
  • When in doubt, open the official website directly instead of using the message link.

How to Stay Safe 🛡️

  • Do not share passwords or OTPs with anyone.
  • Verify unexpected requests through another channel.
  • Use multi-factor authentication.
  • Keep your devices and applications updated.
  • Use security software where appropriate.
  • Report suspicious messages to the relevant organization.

Example 💡

Imagine receiving a message saying that your student account will be permanently disabled unless you immediately click a link and enter your password.

This combination of urgency, fear and a request for credentials can be a warning sign of phishing.

A safer approach is to avoid the link and verify the account status through the institution's official website or contact channel.

Phishing vs Social Engineering

  • Phishing – A specific type of social engineering commonly involving deceptive messages or websites.
  • Social Engineering – A broader category involving psychological manipulation of people.

Ethical Security Awareness 🔐

Cybersecurity professionals may conduct authorized security-awareness exercises to help organizations identify and reduce phishing risks.

Such activities should be performed only with proper authorization, clear rules and appropriate safeguards.

Key Points 📝

  • Phishing attempts to trick users into unsafe actions.
  • Social engineering targets human psychology and trust.
  • Urgency and fear are common warning signs.
  • Never share passwords or OTPs.
  • Always verify suspicious requests.
  • MFA provides additional account protection.
  • Security-awareness testing requires authorization.
